# Main-Shot Adjudication

Use this protocol before locking or revising any source scene's main shots.

## Hold the governing principle

- Make every decision serve the story.
- Treat a main shot as a concrete, drawable image that the story cannot lose or demote to background without material damage to the intended audience experience.
- Let one source scene contain as many main shots as its irreducible story process requires. Never assign a fixed quota or assume one scene has one main shot.
- Distinguish a required story-image proposition from its camera implementation. Test the proposition first; then select a concrete subject, action, change, result, viewpoint, shot size, composition, and movement.
- Let one indispensable process form a main-shot chain when expectation, attempt, obstruction, decision, cost, reaction, and result cannot be compressed without loss.
- Treat extra angles, safety coverage, spatial bridges, repeated reactions, and aesthetic alternatives as supporting shots unless they add a distinct indispensable story or audience-state change.

## Lock the evidence packet

Give every blind agent only the same raw packet:

- the exact source scene, heading, boundary, and order;
- the inherited work definition;
- confirmed story facts and constraints;
- the user's explicit emphasis and weakening instructions, if any.

Do not include another agent's candidates, labels, reasons, or preferred answer. Do not rewrite the scene while extracting evidence.

## Run the blind pass

Use at least three independent subagents plus one coordinator:

1. **Selector A:** derive the scene's story spine and propose concrete main-shot candidates.
2. **Selector B:** independently repeat the task without seeing A.
3. **Negative auditor:** independently list easily missed causes, turns, consequences, emotional or viewpoint changes, prohibited overemphasis, and likely decorative false positives.
4. **Coordinator:** distribute the identical packet, preserve independence, and abstain from proposing a preferred set during the blind pass.

Require each selector to record for every candidate:

- exact source evidence;
- concrete visible subject, action, change, or result;
- single story job;
- the audience-state change: knowledge, expectation, emotion, alignment, tension, viewpoint, or lived process;
- why the content must dominate attention rather than remain incidentally visible;
- the material loss caused by deletion or demotion;
- its relation to other candidates: independent, sequential, dependent, payoff, or true alternative.

Reject `更电影`、`更有氛围`、`更高级`、`更有冲击力` and personal taste as evidence.

## Build the adversarial slate

- Merge the blind outputs into an anonymous union of story-image propositions. Keep materially different candidates separate.
- Group candidates only when source fact, viewing moment, viewpoint, audience-state change, emotional function, and emphasis level are all equivalent.
- Do not group two stages merely because they concern the same fact. Suspense and reveal, action and consequence, audience knowledge and character recognition, or attempt and failure may remain separate main shots.
- Send the anonymous slate back for confrontation. Every candidate must receive a challenge from an agent other than its proposer.

Apply these attacks:

1. **Source test:** identify the exact source authority; reject invention or unsupported emphasis.
2. **Cold deletion:** freeze the rest of the sequence and remove the candidate. Name the lost cause, action, turn, result, character meaning, emotional trajectory, suspense, viewpoint, process weight, or emphasis hierarchy.
3. **Group deletion:** remove the whole similarity group to expose combination redundancy that single deletion misses.
4. **Existing-equivalent test:** identify an already proposed concrete image that carries the same function with equal clarity, timing, viewpoint, and emphasis. Alternative camera techniques alone do not make the story-image proposition dispensable.
5. **Merge test:** merge neighboring candidates only when both remain separately readable and neither falls from dominant viewing to incidental background.
6. **Aesthetic-stripping test:** remove claims about lens, movement, beauty, and production value; state the remaining story necessity.

After any deletion, merge, or duty transfer, recheck the whole source scene: entry state, cause, development, decisive turn, ending state, explicit emphasis, explicit weakening, continuity, and duplicate meaning.

## Adjudicate without voting

Do not use majority vote, average score, confidence totals, or consensus as the decision rule. One source-grounded counterexample outweighs three unsupported approvals.

Resolve candidates in this order:

1. Preserve source scene boundary, facts, and order.
2. Preserve every indispensable cause, action, decisive turn, and consequence.
3. Preserve the intended character meaning, audience alignment, emotional trajectory, suspense, viewpoint, and process weight.
4. Give user-approved emphasis enough differentiated process coverage; give weakened material the minimum coverage required for comprehension.
5. Keep only candidates with a distinct visible story job or distinct indispensable stage.
6. Among equally complete sets, reject redundant propositions; choose camera implementations by story clarity, continuity, and emphasis rather than by vote.

Accept a final main shot only when it has source evidence, a concrete visible carrier, a distinct audience-state change, an answered adversarial challenge, and a recorded deletion or merge loss. A main shot may carry several compatible story duties. Several main shots may carry different indispensable stages of one process.

If evidence cannot resolve a material dispute about story emphasis, present the competing interpretations and request the user's authorial decision. If the dispute is only lens, composition, movement, or taste, label the versions as equivalent coverage choices rather than competing story necessities.

## Record the compact audit

Do not dump agent chatter. For each source scene, retain:

| Item | Required record |
| --- | --- |
| Story spine | Entry → trigger → development → decisive turn → ending state |
| Final main shots | `M01...`: concrete visible image in source order |
| Story and audience job | What changes in the event and in the audience |
| Emphasis reason | Why it must dominate attention |
| Adversarial proof | What deletion, group deletion, or merge would materially damage |
| Relation | Independent shot, indispensable chain stage, or equivalent alternative |
| Rejected candidates | Merge, support, delete, or unresolved, with one-line reason |

After locking the set, design supporting shots around it. Spend coverage on what the audience must notice and experience; compress, background, or omit what should be weakened, without breaking causal comprehension.

## Fail the adjudication

Mark the main-shot judgment invalid when any of these occurs:

- one agent selects and finalizes the set;
- blind agents see another agent's candidates before submitting their own;
- vote count or confidence replaces source-grounded argument;
- a main shot lacks a concrete drawable carrier or exact source basis;
- the set preserves plot summary but destroys emotion, process, suspense, viewpoint, or approved emphasis;
- two candidates differ only by angle, shot size, or camera movement and are counted as two necessities;
- a merge leaves one required event merely visible in the background;
- deletion or duty transfer is judged locally without rechecking the whole scene;
- the process fixes a main-shot count in advance.
